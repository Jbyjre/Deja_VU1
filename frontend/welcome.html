Why it exists

The questions you walk over to the printer to answer.

The printer already publishes the answers. Deja Vu1 reads them for every U1 you run and puts them on one screen, on your phone, or in a message.

Is it still printing?

Every printer updates four times a second. Needs you ranks what wants a person across the whole farm: a jam, a paused print, a finished plate, an idle printer with work waiting. Automations send it to ntfy, Discord or Telegram.

Will this file print on a U1?

The pre-flight check flags extrusion outside the 270 × 270 × 270 mm build volume, unknown toolheads and unsafe temperatures, with line numbers. MakerWorld, NexPrint and OrcaSlicer projects convert for the U1 in one click, multi-plate projects included.

Is the right filament loaded?

Four toolheads mean four chances to load the wrong spool. Deja Vu1 compares the colour each toolhead was sliced for with what the printer reports is loaded, and stops the start on a mismatch, before you spend the time and the filament.

Is it safe to press print?

Confirm Print pulls it together: pre-flight, filament match, nozzles, spool weight, maintenance, time, grams and cost. The server checks again at the moment of starting and refuses in words if something changed, even when you start from your phone.

What's inside

One dashboard instead of five tabs and a walk to the printer.

Watch

  • Live, four times a secondPushed over a WebSocket; it falls back to asking once a second if a network blocks that, and says which.
  • Needs youEvery printer ranked by what wants a person: faults, paused or finished prints, idle printers with work waiting.
  • Whole fleetState, progress, docks, temperatures and a health score for every printer, and when each will finish.
  • Digital twinA live 3D model of the printer, or an engineering drawing: the bed, the toolhead, the parts on the plate, the four docks and the spools, moving as it prints.
  • Dock statusWhich of the four toolheads is ready, heating, in use or faulted, on screen and on LED rings or a WLED strip if you add them.

Print

  • Start from your phoneReview pre-flight and Confirm Print checks before starting.
  • Skip one objectWhen one part on a full plate fails, skip that one and let the rest finish.
  • Prepare, slice, inspectEdit objects and profiles in one workspace, slice with an external engine, then inspect layers, tools, speed and events.
  • Honest controlsA command the printer refuses is shown as refused, in words, never as done.

Make

  • Photo-to-Print StudioMake four-tool colour tiles, measured-swatch layered models or image reliefs. Physical colour accuracy needs calibration.
  • MakerWorld, NexPrint and Orca to U1Converts supported projects for the U1 and separates a selected plate for slicing. Complex vendor paint, textures and settings remain limited.
  • View on your deskPlaces a model on your real desk at its printed size, through an Android phone's camera.

Run

  • AutomationsWhen this happens, do that: notify, set a light, pause the print. Every firing logged with what really happened.
  • Notificationsntfy, Discord or Telegram, for the events you pick in Automations. Urgent rules bypass quiet hours; delivery failures are reported.
  • Smart homeWLED lights and Home Assistant sensors, through their own APIs.
  • App-data recoveryValidate a backup and restore into a separate copy, with automatic actions disabled.
  • Cost per printFilament by material plus electricity, as the print runs.
  • Ten gamesFor while a long print runs. They pause when you look away.

Where it stands

What's checked, and what isn't yet.

You should know exactly what you're running. This is the same status the README keeps.

Checked

  • The printer connection was written from the Moonraker, Klipper and U1 source code Snapmaker publishes, and tested against a stand-in Moonraker built from that source, over real network connections.
  • Automated tests run with nothing but Python; see the repository for current results.
  • Workspace checks passed in Chromium at 1366 and 390 pixels: editing, profiles, actual slicing, preview and Confirm Print entry.
  • Different physical-head heights were verified in actual Linux engine output. Backup recovery and its generated Linux launcher were exercised.
  • WLED, Home Assistant, ntfy, Discord and Telegram checked against each service's own source or documentation.

Not yet

  • It has not talked to a physical U1. The first real connection is where any difference from the published source will show up. The printer's Details panel and the server log are built to make that easy to debug.
  • The LED dock rings and the optical colour sensor have tested logic waiting on physical parts.
  • No physical mixed-nozzle print, measured colour accuracy or real-phone performance is verified. Windows/macOS engine and recovery setup remain untested.
  • Representative converted plates passed the Linux engine; full vendor paint/texture fidelity remains incomplete. Heavy-model rendering remains slow in software graphics.

Your U1 printer

This public website plays a demo recording. It cannot control your printer. Real readings need your own running copy.

  1. On your computer. Install Python 3.8+, download the ZIP from GitHub and extract it. Double-click the Windows, Mac or Linux launcher. Keep that window open and the computer awake.
  2. Connect your printer. Open Set up & help, choose Connect my printer, then Find printers or enter its address. Test and connect. Keep Demo data off for real readings.
  3. On your phone at home. Use the same Wi-Fi and open the computer’s “From a phone” address from the launcher, such as http://192.168.1.20:8000. Pairing is optional, not a login.
  4. Keep it on your home screen. Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen, or Chrome’s menu → Add to Home screen. HTTP may offer only a shortcut; secure HTTPS enables full installation and the offline explanation. Offline never means live readings.
  5. Away from home. Use private Tailscale Serve, or a Cloudflare tunnel with Access protection configured first. Deja Vu1 has no login. Do not forward its port through your router.

Exact setup and firewall steps · Private remote access

An optional name stays only in your browser. App update checks are off by default; enable them in Settings if you want them. Neither your name nor printer details are sent in an update check.
